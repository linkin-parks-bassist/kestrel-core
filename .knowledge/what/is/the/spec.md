Core is a programmable 16-bit fixed-point DSP with two pipelines, ordered commit, resources and SPI programming. Headers select 256 blocks, 16 channels, 112.5 MHz and 44.1 kHz; these source facts are not complete acceptance requirements.

David selects a polynomial-only extraction of the existing filter engine as the default, retaining the working Chamberlin SVF and keeping the original general filter optional. ENABLE_FILTER, ENABLE_POLYNOMIAL and ENABLE_SVF independently include or exclude those units. Preserve polynomial coefficient programming through the existing commands and preserve SVF runtime parameters, stateful update/read pairing and private once-per-sample ordinal state, without configuration handles. External control-written damping, replacement recurrence and broader local history/state architecture are not selected. Polynomial approximation remains important for audio-rate functions such as exponentials.

The canonical descriptor-driven joint argument-format resolver and signed-Q15 cutoff remain the paired compiler/RTL contract. Require nonnegative cutoff handling, fixed cutoff scale, independently formatted signed damping and matching coefficient widening/product normalization. Descriptor send transforms and generic saturation provide expression bounds; channels supply corresponding DSP words. Do not shortcut the resolver with instruction-specific evaluator or wire-writer branches. The exact historical failure of range guarantees after general readbacks remains uncertain; the filter-engine owner governs checked behavior, numerical considerations and limitations.

Add read32 as a 24-bit, word-aligned addressed SPI read, dispatched outside the controller and returning four bytes through the existing response mechanism. Hardcoded address 0 is a 32-bit magic word and address 4 is the compiled capability mask. The source contract selects magic 0x4b455354 and bits 0/1/2 for filter/polynomial/SVF. Keep future narrower read24/read16/read8 on the same word-aligned address scheme. This begins capability discovery without replacing the other commands. Firmware should ultimately know its partner image's capabilities and reject unsupported instructions or implement an applicable alternative, such as lowering a supported classic biquad through SVF instructions. Exact lowering, encoding-range feasibility and acceptance remain to be defined; do not claim all biquads fit the finite SVF.

David accepts the current shared, serialized filter/polynomial/SVF branch for now, but intends polynomial processing to have a separate linear instruction branch. Each branch must remain strictly in order: the commit mechanism cannot repair within-branch overtaking. ENABLE_* options should ultimately control populated execution branches. For excluded operations, David favors doing nothing, potentially through a direct path to the corresponding commit stage; this is tentative until commit-ID issuance, retirement and suppressed channel/accumulator writes are specified. Dropping a packet that owns a commit ID would wedge subsequent retirement. Do not implement this branch redesign merely as a result-stream merger or silently discard issued commit IDs.

David is happy with the current ISA, including the delay instructions, and authorizes cultivating the verified effect library. Exact-offset buffer reads are unnecessary. Fractional-delay interpolation remains a future implementation feature. Current delay modulation sounds good for his tested flanging case; recommend against long-delay modulation due to quantization, without forbidding it or promising good results in 24-bit mode. Do not use the old general-filter engine for this library; its optional implementation may be revisited on a larger FPGA with 24-bit audio. These decisions do not cancel the deferred execution-path optimizations below.

David's other intended RTL changes are:

1. Reverse operand-fetch order to C, then B, then A. This is his highest-ROI immediate RTL improvement: most instructions need A, while most do not need C, so an A dependency currently blocks at an unnecessarily early stage. The objective is instruction throughput in cycles. David identifies two potential gains: A has two additional stages of travel in which its producer can become ready before fetch, and an instruction stalled specifically on A has two fewer operand-fetch stages left after resolution. He anticipates potentially four cycles saved in the typical A-dependent case and sizeable throughput improvement; the four-cycle figure and his informal estimate that A stalls dominate are hypotheses, not measured facts. Complete instruction/dependency-chain timelines establish the combined saving without double-counting overlapping time. The wiring is now implemented; the current synthetic actual-core comparison shows two cycles saved per A dependency, with results and limits owned by how/to/measure/core/instruction/throughput.md. Preserve dependency correctness, unused-operand handling, backpressure and ordered commit.
2. Use the hardware DSP barrel shifters instead of separately pipelining shifting within the MADD path, aiming to shorten MADD. Exact primitive mapping, arithmetic semantics and latency need qualification.
3. Deferred: split MOV and ADD/SUB out of the full MADD path, bypassing redundant multiply and shift stages. David expects substantial throughput gains because these instructions are common; the magnitude remains unmeasured. MOV may go directly to commit, while ADD/SUB need a shorter arithmetic path. Router recognition of MADD x * 1 + 0 is a proposed MOV mechanism, not a settled encoding or equivalence rule. Qualify arithmetic, shift and saturation semantics, hazards, backpressure and ordered retirement before choosing the paths. This work is explicitly deferred; fetch-order changes and cycle measurements come first.
4. Reconsider the execution-branch choices after the barrel-shifter work. The replacement branch architecture is not yet specified.
5. During pipeline transitions, use envelopes to compensate for phase cancellation: when the mixed result is substantially quieter than the sum of envelopes, gently increase gain to avoid audible ducking. Envelope definition, normalization, threshold, gain bounds and dynamics remain open.
6. Add postprocessing cleanup filters, gentle AGC and a declicker. David's long-planned declicker is unwritten; algorithm, triggering, stage order, latency and acceptance remain open. Filter cutoffs/topology and AGC dynamics/headroom also remain open.

7. Replace system-clock-sampled SPI with a proper SCK-clocked frontend and an explicit crossing into the main SYS_CLK domain. David reports reliability falling off as SCK is increased and expects this change to permit higher reliable SCK frequency; the threshold and gain remain unmeasured. Preserve command/response semantics while defining receive buffering/overflow, reset/enable, CS/framing and MISO/status return crossings. Validate independent clock phases, rates and duty cycles, then qualify reliable SCK limits on hardware. Priority is not yet assigned.

David now prioritizes timing closure after seeing the routed polynomial/SVF/read32 report. Preserve the configured 112.5 MHz clock and constraints, establish the actual critical paths, and verify dependency/retirement behavior and throughput alongside timing changes. The previous deferral no longer governs this work.

The shared contract is owned by the superproject's what/is/the/effect/verification/specification.md; one-pipeline simulation does not itself change the product's pipeline count.

The integrated-board converter and FPGA-owned audio-clock migration remains governed by the superproject hardware specification. Further RTL intentions and formal acceptance criteria remain to be elicited; do not infer them from incomplete existing modules.
